AutomationBench environment exploration policy.

Use this as generic execution guidance for workflow automation tasks. It must
not contain task-specific entity names, IDs, payloads, or teacher traces.

Before taking side effects:
- Explore the environment enough to understand the current workflow. When the
  user asks for a standard process, latest guideline, required format, or normal
  channel behavior, do not infer from memory. Retrieve the relevant policy
  source, recent examples, or system records first.
- Exploration is read-only. Do not use write, create, update, post, send,
  delete, execute, or mutation endpoints to announce that you are searching or
  to probe the environment. Status messages are side effects and are forbidden.
  Use only search, list, get, fetch, history, and read endpoints until the final
  payload and target have been resolved.
- Discover the available read and write APIs before mutating. Prefer
  `api_search` with `top_k: 10` for the first search in each relevant system.
- For notification tasks, inspect the target channel, inbox, knowledge base, or
  policy store for recent canonical examples before composing the notification.
  Recent canonical examples usually teach exact prefixes, labels, separators,
  capitalization, and amount formatting.
- For Slack notification tasks, first discover/read channel history or message
  search endpoints such as conversations history or message search. Do not call
  chat post/update/delete endpoints until the final notification text is ready.
- Retrieve enough recent examples to identify a repeated norm. For channel
  history, prefer at least 50 messages when allowed, or use message search for
  likely format terms from the task such as status labels, announcement words,
  channel names, or object types. Do not treat one informal or decorative recent
  message as canonical if older examples show a repeated structured pattern.
- If the user references "latest process guidelines", search communication and
  policy surfaces for guideline messages. Treat internal business policy and
  recent canonical examples as stronger than external advice, informal notes,
  or per-record preferences.
- Build the final mutation payload from visible evidence. Preserve exact
  strings, channel names, record IDs, owner names, labels, separators, dates,
  and amounts from authoritative sources.
- Resolve foreign keys and user IDs into human-readable names before composing
  user-facing notifications. If a source record exposes fields such as OwnerId,
  UserId, AssigneeId, AccountId, ContactId, channel ID, or similar identifiers,
  fetch the referenced object or search the relevant users/people endpoint
  before writing the final message.
- If sources conflict, prefer explicit user targets and formal internal policy
  over source-record preferences, external suggestions, and intuitive defaults.
  If the conflict affects the mutation, inspect adjacent evidence before acting.
- A user-specified target channel, recipient, object, or workflow is a hard
  requirement unless a formal internal policy explicitly overrides it. A
  customer/account-specific preference is evidence to consider, not authority to
  override the user's requested target.
- Keep a private checklist of required side effects and exact substrings before
  each write. The write payload should satisfy the checklist exactly.
- After each mutation, verify by fetching or searching the changed object or
  target channel. Empty mutation responses such as `{}` or 204-style no-content
  responses often mean success; verify instead of repeating the same write.
- Final answer only after all required side effects are complete and verified.
